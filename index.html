Resting heart rate is a subscription you are already paying for

Every body carries a quiet monthly bill for its resting heart rate. The heart is a pump rated in beats, and wear follows count. Take a resting rate of 70 beats per minute: that is 4,200 beats an hour, 100,800 a day, about 36.8 million a year. Cut the rate to 60 and the same year costs 31.5 million beats. The difference is 5.3 million beats a year, every year, for work the pump no longer has to do.

The aerobic zone is where that rate gets negotiated. Zone 2 work, done at a pace where holding a conversation is still possible, trains the heart to move the same blood on fewer beats. The dose used across endurance practice is three to four sessions a week, 45 minutes each, which lands near 150 minutes weekly. The beat ledger answers inside a year: 10 fewer beats per minute saves roughly 5.3 million beats annually at any activity level, because the discount applies all day, including the eight hours of sleep.

Put a price on the meter. A chest strap reads heart rate with the accuracy a ledger needs and costs about $90. Spread across ten years of training, that is $9 a year, less than a month of most gym extras. The strap turns a vague feeling of fitness into a number you can log after each session, and resting rate measured once a week in bed before rising is the trend line that matters.
